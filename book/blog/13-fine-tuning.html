When prompting is not enough

When fine-tuning is worth it, how to build the dataset, how to train locally or remotely, and how to prove it helped.

2 min read · Based on Chapter 13 of Building AI Applications

Fine-tuning is powerful, and often unnecessary. Chapter 13 explains when it is worth the effort and how to do it properly when it is.

Fine-tune for behaviour, not knowledge

Fine-tuning changes how a model responds: format, style, tone, and consistency on a narrow task. It is a poor way to teach facts, which change and need citations. Use RAG for knowledge.

Good reasons to fine-tune:

  • a narrow, high-volume task where a smaller tuned model can replace a large general one;
  • a strict output format or domain style that prompting cannot hold reliably;
  • latency or cost targets that a large model cannot meet.

Beat the baseline first

Before training anything, build the strongest prompted baseline you can: good instructions, examples, retrieval and the right model. Fine-tuning must beat that baseline on your golden dataset. Otherwise it is cost without benefit.

The dataset decides the result

Training data comes from human-in-the-loop corrections, production logs, expert-written examples, distillation from a larger model and careful synthetic generation. Whatever the source, it must be:

  • clean and correctly labelled;
  • deduplicated and balanced across case types;
  • validated by experts;
  • versioned;
  • leakage-free, with no test cases in the training set.

Train locally or remotely

  • Locally: Unsloth makes LoRA and QLoRA fine-tuning fast and memory-efficient on a single GPU.
  • Remotely: Hugging Face, GPU clouds and managed fine-tuning services scale to larger models and datasets.

LoRA adapters are small, so you can keep several and switch between them.

Test before you deploy

Evaluate the tuned model on:

  • task performance against the baseline on the golden dataset;
  • general capability, to check that the model has not forgotten how to follow instructions;
  • robustness and safety, including adversarial inputs and refusal behaviour;
  • efficiency: latency, throughput and cost.

Key takeaways

  • Fine-tune for behaviour and format; use RAG for knowledge.
  • A strong prompted baseline must be beaten first.
  • Dataset quality decides the result.
